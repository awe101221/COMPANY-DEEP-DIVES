## 1. Bottom line

Q2 2026 (released 2026-08-05) is the last material company event. MELI printed a first-ever $10.2bn revenue quarter at 50% USD / 43% FX-neutral growth, while operating income fell 17% YoY and operating margin compressed 543 bps (calculated) to 6.7%. ## 6. Risks and disconfirming evidence

**Strongest case against the “investments will pay” conclusion:**

1. **Negative incremental EBIT** is now two quarters old (Q1 EBIT −20% YoY; Q2 −17%). Management has used this script since 2025. The burden of proof is on *payback timing*, not on narrative coherence.
2. **90+ DPD of 18.7%** vs advertised 15–90 NPL of 7.0%. Analyst Kaio Prato (UBS) flagged 90-day NPL deterioration on the call. Giménez attributed it to growth-rate mix and a late-2025 vintage that defaulted more than expected, then was throttled. That is a **plausible but unverified** vintage story.
3. **Card NIMAL −2.5%** while cards are 47% of the book and unused lines are $14bn. If issuance stays at 2.6mn/quarter, the blended book can stay negative even if old Brazil cohorts are fine.
4. **Mexico** sequential DC margin −4 ppts; tax reform still a headwind; World Cup hit June–July (management). Acquiring device losses are partly structural (chip prices; competitors did not raise prices).
5. **Argentina** FXN growth slowing (revenue FXN 65% → 48%); 33.8% y/y inflation; DC dollars already falling YoY.
6. **Cash conversion:** H1 adj. FCF $158mn on $883mn net income. Credit is eating cash faster than earnings.

**Disconfirming evidence *for* the bear case (i.e., why the bear may be wrong):** 15–90 NPL at lows; Brazil consumer NIMAL recovered QoQ after a flagged Q1 provision spike; management has previously cut lines when vintages slipped; ads and ecosystemic-user math, if true, can justify spending.

## 7. One deep-research finding

**Selected open question:** *Is credit-card NIMAL of −2.5% a temporary cohort-mix effect, or a structural drag as cards become ~half the book?*

**Finding (see `credit-deep-dive-2026-08-16.md`):**  
The −2.5% print is **consistent with vintage dilution** if Brazil’s historical 12–18 month cohort breakeven still holds — issuance jumped from 1.6mn to 2.6mn cards, and Giménez said Mexico paybacks are *better* than Brazil. That is the bull reading.

The 10-Q does **not** confirm it. What the 10-Q *does* show: cards are already 47% of a $16.4bn book; card allowance coverage is only 18.8% vs 33–40% on consumer/merchant (so the card book is underwritten as safer — or under-reserved); unused commitments of $14.0bn are a second, larger credit exposure; 90–360 DPD is 18.7% of *all* loans; H1 card provision charge was $758mn vs $471mn a year earlier. Until MELI discloses **cohort NIMAL curves or 90+ by product**, the “temporary mix” claim remains a **management statement**, not a verified fact.

**Working conclusion (55% confidence):** card NIMAL stays negative through at least Q4 2026 if issuance remains ≥2.5mn/quarter; the *stock* outcome depends on whether ecosystem LTV (higher GMV, principality, ads) exceeds the credit P&L hole. Falsifier: two more quarters of card NIMAL ≤ −2% *and* 15–90 card NPL > 6%, or unused commitments > $18bn without a matching rise in card NIMAL.
