# Deep research: credit-card NIMAL −2.5%

**Question:** Is Q2 2026 credit-card NIMAL of −2.5% a temporary cohort-mix effect from faster issuance, or a structural drag as cards become ~half the book?  
**Decision it informs:** whether 2026–27 earnings can recover without a volume crash, and whether unused card lines are an off-balance time bomb.  
**As-of:** 2026-08-16.

## Arithmetic

**Stock of risk, 2026-06-30:**

- Funded card book $7.7bn + unused lines $14.0bn = **$21.7bn** card credit exposure.
- Group funded book $16.4bn. Unused lines are almost as large as the entire funded book.
- If even 5% of unused lines are drawn into a bad vintage, that is ~$700mn of new problem loans — same order of magnitude as a quarter of PDA.

**Flow of losses, H1 2026:**

- Card provision charge $758mn / average card gross ~(5,656+7,676)/2 = $6,666mn → **~22.7% annualized** provision rate on cards (my calculation; not NIMAL).
- Card write-offs $483mn / same average → **~14.5% annualized** write-off rate.
- Group write-offs $1,370mn / avg gross $14,442mn → **~19.0% annualized**.

These rates are normal for high-yield LatAm unsecured **if** yields are 40%+ and NIMAL is 20%. They are **not** compatible with a *card* NIMAL of −2.5% unless (a) new cohorts have near-zero net interest after losses, or (b) funding + rewards + interchange mix is worse than consumer loans. Both can be true at once.

**Mix math (inference):**  
If non-card NIMAL is roughly mid-20s to 30s (letter: “other loans continue to show strong profitability”) and cards are 47% at −2.5%, the blended 20.7% is internally consistent. Example: 0.53 × 41% + 0.47 × (−2.5%) ≈ 20.6%. I do **not** have the official non-card NIMAL; this is a check that the mix story is *arithmetically possible*, not proof.

**Issuance dilution (inference):**  
+1.0mn extra cards in the quarter. If a new card starts at, say, −15% to −25% first-year NIMAL (unknown — **not disclosed**) and seasoned cards are +8% to +12% (also unknown), a 60% jump in issuance can move the blended card NIMAL from 0% to −2.5% without any vintage deterioration. That is why management’s story is *plausible*. It is not evidenced.

## 15–90 vs 90+

The letter markets 15–90 NPL of 7.0% / 4.6% cards, “near historic lows.”

The 10-Q aging (all products, not by product):

| Bucket | $ mn | % of gross |
|---|---:|---:|
| Current | 11,737 | 71.7% |
| 1–14 | 435 | 2.7% |
| 15–90 | 1,143 | 7.0% |
| 90–360 | 3,060 | 18.7% |
| Write-off policy | 360 days | — |

UBS (Kaio Prato) said 90-day NPL “had quite a meaningful deterioration.” Giménez: the 90+ ratio mixes products, improves automatically when you originate, and a late-2025 vintage defaulted more than expected, after which they slowed that product. CFO: short-duration products leave good payers quickly and keep defaulters to 360 days, distorting 90+.

**My read:** both can be true. A 18.7% 90+ stock on a 360-day write-off policy is **not automatically a crisis** — it is partly a definition. But it is also **not** “asset quality at all-time lows” in any conventional 90+ sense. Until MELI gives 90+ *by product*, the clean 15–90 print is the number management wants used, and the 10-Q aging is the number a credit analyst should not ignore.

Renegotiations 1.5% of the book (10-Q) — not a hidden modification factory, at least on this disclosure.

## Funding

Q2: $2.1bn invested in the credit book, $560mn of fintech funding (CFO). H1 loans receivable net change −$4.1bn vs +$1.2bn fintech funding in the adj. FCF bridge. The book is **not self-funding**. Customer funds ($16.0bn payable) and restricted cash ($13.1bn) are large but are **liabilities / restricted**, not free capital. Net debt (mgmt) $6.4bn, +$1.7bn in six months.

Mexico $150mn and Argentina $100mn true-sale facilities are rounding error vs a $16bn book.

## Strongest counterargument

If Brazil’s 12–18 month breakeven has been “pretty consistent,” and Mexico is better, then −2.5% is the price of 2.6mn cards that turn marketplace users into ecosystemic users whose contribution profit “multiplies.” In that world, looking at card NIMAL in isolation is a category error — the return shows up in GMV, ads, and retention.

**What would prove that:** cohort curves, or two years of card NIMAL > 5% after issuance normalizes, plus stable 15–90 *and* 90+ by product. None of that is public.

## Conclusion

- **55%:** −2.5% is mostly vintage mix and stays negative through Q4 2026 if issuance remains ≥2.5mn/quarter.
- **25%:** it is the start of a worse card cycle (late-2025 vintage + Argentina + unused-line draw).
- **20%:** it mean-reverts faster than I think (Mexico economics + throttle).

**Confidence:** 50%. One 10-Q and one call. No cohort tape.
