## Bottom line (as of 2026-08-17)

MELI is compounding a hard-to-replicate Commerce + Fintech ecosystem at high volume growth while **deliberately suppressing near-term operating margin**. Q2’26 net revenues & financial income were **$10.2B (+50% YoY USD / +43% FX-neutral)** with operating margin **6.7%** (−550 bps YoY). Engagement, ecosystemic users, ads, and credit-card principality support a credible bull path; credit mix dilution, reinvestment intensity, LatAm FX/macro, and competitive response support a material bear path.

**Probability-weighted view:** modest expected upside vs ~$1,845 if base/bull engagement economics mature as management describes, with a fat left tail if credit or margin wars break. This is **not** a buy/sell call; it is a sourced scenario framework.

## Operating driver map (what the cases turn on)

1. **Engagement → lifetime value (Commerce + Fintech flywheel)**  
   - Reported fact (Q2’26 letter): ecosystemic users +37% YoY; vs marketplace-only, +70% GMV/user and +55% items/user; vs fintech-only, ~+90% TPV/user and >2× AUM/user; contribution profit per ecosystemic user is “multiples” of the sum of single-vertical users (**management claim** — not independently audited).  
   - MELI+ subscribers +72% YoY; cardholders 2–3× more likely to remain ecosystemic (**management claim**).

2. **Brazil free-shipping threshold (structural demand vs margin tax)**  
   - One year after lowering BR free-shipping threshold: FXN GMV +39%, items +56%; conversion +1.1 ppt YoY even on anniversary; DAU/MAU inflected; free & slow shipments variable contribution-positive across half of R$19–R$79 ASP ranges (**management claim** with partial unit-econ disclosure).

3. **Credit scale and mix**  
   - Portfolio >$16B (+75% YoY); consolidated NIMAL 20.7% (−2 ppt YoY) on mix shift to cards (43%→47%); card NIMAL −2.5% on issuance (2.6M new cards vs 1.6M); 15–90 NPL 7.0% overall / 4.6% cards near lows (**reported**).  
   - Bull requires card cohorts to follow historical 12–18 month NIMAL breakeven path (asserted in Q4’25 letter). Bear is a credit cycle and/or funding-cost shock while the book is still growing faster than revenue.

4. **Ads and monetization**  
   - Ads FXN net revenue +62% YoY; >10% LatAm digital ad share for first time (**reported / management**). High-margin overlay if traffic and seller tools keep converting.

5. **Geography**  
   - Brazil is the growth engine (USD rev +59% / FXN +42% in Q2’26). Mexico solid but tax-reform headwind (FXN GMV +26%). Argentina FXN GMV +38% against soft consumption; USD rev only +20% (FX headwind).

**Variant perception (inference):** The market is paying a growth multiple while margins are intentionally depressed. The bull case is that today’s margin is an investment, not a new normal; the bear case is that competition and credit make mid-single-digit margins structural and that ~50× earnings already embeds too much success. Consensus sell-side targets (~$2,250 on Yahoo as of mid-Aug 2026) are **third-party** and not used as primary evidence.

### What appears priced in (inference, not a formal reverse DCF)

At ~$1,845 / ~50× TTM earnings with **6.7%** op. margin, the market appears to require **either** (a) continued ~30%+ top-line growth with eventual margin repair, **or** (b) acceptance of lower margins offset by fintech/credit scale. A clean reverse DCF is deferred until credit funding, customer funds, and restricted cash are modeled explicitly (open question).

## Valuation notes and method limits

- Cases use **operating-driver scenarios** and illustrative exit equity ranges, not a full three-statement model.  
- Do **not** treat company Adjusted Free Cash Flow as unlevered FCF: H1’26 adj. FCF was **$158M** after large credit book growth; GAAP CFO was **$5.7B**, dominated by customer-fund and working-capital dynamics.  
- Enterprise value must eventually net: cash, restricted cash, investments, loans payable (corporate vs fintech), leases, funds payable to customers, and credit receivables. Current “~$100B EV” is a **shortcut**.  
- Argentina inflation/FX and hyperinflation accounting remain material translation risks (see risk register).
