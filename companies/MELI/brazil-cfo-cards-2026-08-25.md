## 2. The provision vs 15–90 tension

This is the only credit mechanism that is not already in the letter.

- **Reported fact:** card 15–90 NPL **4.6%** vs group **7.0%**. Cards look *better* on the advertised early-delinquency print.
- **Lima claim:** cards require *higher* loss provisions than the other two lines, and that is why direct profitability is lower.
- **Letter claim:** card NIMAL −2.5% is “not because of a deterioration in asset quality”; it “simply reflects the significant step-up in issuance.”

These can be jointly true under CECL / vintage accounting:

1. Day-1 expected-credit-loss provisions on 2.6mn new cards hit NIMAL before those vintages enter 15–90.
2. Cards can have a higher lifetime loss *rate* (or a higher provision *dollar* load because they are 47% of the book) while still printing a lower current 15–90 than consumer/merchant.
3. “Other loans” can still be “strongly profitable” (letter) if their NIMAL stays high even after a higher 15–90.

They can also be jointly false if Lima is collapsing “higher PDA dollars because the card book grew” into “higher PDA rate.” Folha does not quote a product-level coverage or PDA/average-book figure.

**Working inference (not a fact):** the interview is additional management-claim evidence for the **vintage / acquisition-product** side of question #1. It is not independent cohort evidence. The 10-Q 90–360 DPD of **18.7%** of gross loans (Note 4, 2026-06-30) remains the disconfirming aging print the letter and the interview both ignore.

## 3. Funding pass-through

Lima: where risk is higher, funding cost is higher, and MELI “repassa isso” (passes it through) to protect against product risk.

**Implication if true:** card NIMAL weakness is not a funding-spread surprise. It is price minus CECL minus a product-specific cost of funds. Negative NIMAL then means **price + interchange + other card revenue still does not cover CECL + allocated funding** on the current mix.

**What is still unknown:** the internal transfer-price, how much of card yield is interchange vs interest, and whether “repassa” applies to APR, to merchant discount, or to both. Not disclosed.
