| Funds payable to customers | 16.04 | Reported — not a corporate liability in EV |
| Net loans receivable | 12.00 | Operating asset of the lender |

At 10% WACC, street-like revenue fade ($41.6bn → $53.1bn → $129bn by 2035), and base reinvestment (credit still consumes cash early), the price implies a **~17% terminal EBIT margin**. MELI’s peak reported EBIT margin in the 2023–25 10-K history is **14.6% (2023)**. At 9% WACC and bull cash conversion, the implied terminal margin falls to **~13%** — roughly 2024’s 12.7%. If EBIT stays 6.7%, EV is **~$41–47bn** ($720–850/share).

SOTP: credit at 1.2× net book = $14.4bn. Residual **$85.5bn** on an estimated ~$21bn 2026 non-credit revenue base is **~4.1× sales**. That is a high-quality marketplace/ads multiple, not a distressed-reinvestment multiple.

**Working conclusion (55% confidence):** the stock is a duration-and-margin-recovery instrument. Growth is not the scarce assumption; **owner-earnings conversion and terminal margin** are. Falsifier for this valuation reading: two more quarters of adj. FCF run-rate ≥$1.5bn annualized *or* a disclosed path to ≥10% EBIT with stable 15–90 NPL would make today’s multiple less heroic. Opposite falsifier: Q3 EBIT margin ≤5.5% with adj. FCF negative would make even the $1,200 “11% terminal / 10% WACC” case optimistic.
